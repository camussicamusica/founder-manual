# Day 1: the Pareto story and what a Right Hand is

Speaker: Ivan Bunin, co-founder, opening Bootcamp 7. Excerpts are short and verbatim (line number first). Spoken English, so grammar is as transcribed.

## Summary of the origin story
Ivan started freelancing at 16, jumping between entrepreneurs for 400 to 600 dollars a month. In 2018 he answered a job post for an executive assistant. For the first time he was not treated as a disposable VA. He grew from executive assistant to technical team lead to CTO of a small agency (about 6 people, grown to about 60), Sol8.com, which was sold in 2022. After the sale he and Kasim launched Pareto Talent to do the same for others: find strong people who never had the right opportunity and place them with entrepreneurs. The first cohort was 11 people. The first client was Tim Bratton, founder of Rhapsody (per Ivan). Today Ivan cites 100+ founders and 250+ people in the community. The same story appears on the public site; see [[home-page]] and [[pareto-talent-identity]].

## Right Hand versus assistant (the passages founders and talent are told)
Summary: Ivan says "executive assistant" is closest, but Pareto defines the role as a right hand. An assistant waits to be told, finishes a task and stops, works from a job description. A right hand notices what is missing, closes the loop and reports back without being chased, works from the outcome the founder needs, and owns things completely. Ivan's test: if the founder could ask AI for the same thing, the person will be replaced by AI.

- L1425: "we will be sometimes saying the word executive assistant, not a virtual assistant, we don't use this term."
- L1429: "A usual assistant, you just sit down and wait to be told what to do."
- L1437: "if your entrepreneur or employer or company that you work for could ask AI to do the same thing that they're asking you, then sooner than later, you will be replaced by artificial intelligence"
- L1445: "assistant just finishes a task and they stop."
- L1445: "you didn't tell me to check that, I wasn't asked for that"
- L1457: "The assistant works from a job description"
- L1485: "you're noticing what is missing before anyone points in this. You close the loop and report back without being chased."
- L1493: "you need to be able to take something and own it completely."

The public site makes the same contrast in verbatim pairs ([[home-page]]). Brand rule that follows: say "Right Hand" or "executive assistant", never "virtual assistant" ([[brand-voice]], [[CLAUDE]]).
