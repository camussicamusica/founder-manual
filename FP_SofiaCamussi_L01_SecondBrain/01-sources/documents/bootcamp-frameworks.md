# Bootcamp frameworks used in this project

Citation format: D5 L1049 = Day 5 transcript, file line 1049; "Slide N" = Day 10 deck. Speaker is Ivan Bunin throughout. Name normalisations: "Alex Harmozik" is Alex Hormozi; "Cloud" is Claude; "Casim" is Kasim. Applied in [[lead-magnet-brief]], [[personas]] and [[icp]]. Rubric context: [[final-project-guidelines]].

## 1. MAGIC formula (Day 5, L1049-1097; Day 10, Slide 38, L2181)
Used to name an offer. M Magnet (an attractive hook that describes what it is); A Avatar (who it is for, no guesses); G Goal or outcome (the result, stated directly; garbled in the transcript, Hormozi's G is Goal); I Interval (time frame, "in 10 weeks"); C Container (what it is called, for example challenge). Worked name at D5 L1085: "Get your life back from your business in 10 weeks challenge". Mechanics are explained below the name.

## 2. Value Equation (Day 5, L253-349)
Value is the ratio of benefit to cost, where cost includes the cost of not solving the problem. Four levers: dream outcome (push up), likelihood of achievement (push up, via proof), time delay (push down, faster first result), effort and sacrifice (push down, less hassle). Scarcity and urgency must be real (L357-377). Day 10 asks to push it so a free resource "feels like a good deal" (D10 L2181).

## 3. Offer framework (Day 5, L125-141, L237-245, L961-1041)
Five parts in order: audience (ICP), problem or pain, solution, bonuses and value stacking, guarantee. Guarantee types: unconditional, outcome-based, conditional, anti-guarantee. Research the ICP from transcripts, forums and reviews, "not from you guessing" (L413-449). Use AI as a multiplier of what you already brainstormed (L2265-2305).

## 4. Six elements of every ad (Day 8, L1689-1881; Slide 42)
Hook (what stops the person; a pattern interrupt; "Do you need an executive assistant?" is not a hook, L1765-1773) then offer, qualifier (who it is and is not for, L1829-1833), proof, mechanism (comes later, what you actually do), call to action ("Without the call to action, you don't have an ad", L1865-1881). Five ads must be five different messages, each a persona and angle pair, not five hooks on one message (Slide 42; D10 L2313).

## 5. Funnel stages TOFU, MOFU, BOFU (Day 8, L301-723)
TOFU: unaware, educate (YouTube, podcasts, social). MOFU: aware of the problem, not of you (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn ads). BOFU: ready and intent-driven (Google search for the service or brand, referrals, email to the list). "We need all of them" (L605). The rubric asks for ads by funnel stage (Slide 47).

## 6. Landing page rules (Days 5 and 6)
- Day 5 homework: at least 8 sections (hero, social proof, problem, how it works, benefits, offer, guarantees, final CTA), L3389. Final project version: Hero, What's inside, Who it's for, Proof with real Pareto numbers, CTA with the form (Slide 40).
- Hero: the first screen decides stay or scroll; everything on one screen (D6 L9-17, L145-169). Clarity over creativity (D6 L749).
- CTA: highest-contrast element, one primary CTA, never "Submit" (D6 L209-261, L1809-1813).
- Proof: testimonials, reviews, real photos, numbers served (D6 L857-889). Ad and page must match ("continuity", D6 L3393-3429).
- Thank-you page: never skip it; show what happens next, deliver the value at once, handle objections (D6 L3205-3233; D8 L925-993). Qualified goes to booking, not qualified to a thank-you that delivers the resource (Slides 40 and 49).
- Forms: qualifier fields are dropdown or radio with fixed answers (D6 L1473-1509); conditional redirect to the right page (D6 L1829-1845, L2353-2461); test both paths.

## 7. Business map six-stage spine (Day 10 bonus, L305-329; Slide 6)
1 Traffic · 2 Capture · 3 Qualify and nurture · 4 Sales, money, handoff · 5 Onboarding through 90 days · 6 Retain, grow, exit (14 stages in total). Draw from the client's perspective, left to right. The 10-second rule (L473-481): if you cannot name the document, process or link for a step in about ten seconds, it is a gap; mark it and never invent a link. Final project: green is built and linked, red is a gap (D10 L2113).

## 8. SOP sections (Day 9, L3289-3301; Day 3, L109-157)
Definition: a written tutorial someone else can follow; "If you do something twice, then there should be a SOP for that" (D3). Sections as taught: Purpose, Scope, Definitions or acronyms, Procedure (literal steps). Ivan: structures can differ. Final project needs one SOP so the next launch repeats the process (Slide 37; D10 L2149-2157).

## 9. Second Brain (Day 3, L69-89, L401-817, L1229-2049)
This brain follows it: a folder of Markdown files (L685-689) with frontmatter so the AI knows what a file is without reading it (L721-737), wikilinks so related files are discovered (L741-757), and a MOC (map of content) in every folder: the AI reads the MOC, picks a file, reads its frontmatter, follows links (L789-817). Two layers: a context layer (static knowledge) and an action layer (live connectors) (L69-89). The folder lives in the founder's own Drive (D3 L493-501). Day 2 homework asked for three source types: transcripts, documents, public information (D2 L3049-3073).

## 10. ICP and personas (Day 8, L1217-1657)
ICP: "customer that we love, that we want, that we need" (L1217-1241). Pareto's ICP: business owners, about $500K+ revenue, not solo startups (D8 L1357-1365; D3 L1653-1661). Client words: "I'm the bottleneck in my own company" (L1401-1417). Personas by cause: defined by the pain that brings them, not demographics (D8 homework L3061; D10 L2093). Board personas: agency owner, speaker or coach or consultant, mid-size company wanting vetted talent and a replacement guarantee; the fourth was not read out. Applied in [[icp]] and [[personas]].

## 11. Lead magnets (Day 10, L2165-2201; Slides 38-39)
"a resource that somebody wants, and they want it so much that they give you their email address" (L2165-2169). Qualifies the lead, then a next-step offer. Continuity between magnet and main offer (D10 L5381-5421). Examples: [[existing-lead-magnets]].

## 12. Four C's of communication (Day 1, L2153-2253)
Clear (write so you cannot be misunderstood), Concise, Complete (all information needed to act), Call to action (end with a question or next step). Invoice example at L2213-2229. "Communication is the job."

## 13. Close the loop (Day 1, L2381-2437; Day 9, L41, L121)
A task is not done until the person who asked knows it is done; each task is an open loop; "your message should be the last one" (D1 L2393). Day 9: "You're being hired to close the loops." This is the behavior Pareto sells in a Right Hand; see [[day-01-pareto-story]].
